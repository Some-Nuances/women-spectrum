How to read these results. Your answers are placed on twelve themes, each between two opposite positions. The percentage says how closely your answers resemble each thinker's position across all these themes. 100% would be a perfect copy, which never happens: from 50% the closeness is real, and from 65% it is strong. The words next to the number (“very close”, “close”, “fairly close”, “distant”) help you read it. The position attributed to each thinker is my simplified reading of her work, not an official measure: her ideas are more nuanced and have evolved. Being close to a thinker doesn't mean agreeing on everything. Positions on the last four themes (race, power, family, women's responsibility) are less documented than on the first eight: read them as rough markers. The theme summaries are my own paraphrases; the quoted excerpts come from authors whose work is in the public domain (free translations). “Partial” profiles rely only on the themes where their positions are known, and are excluded from the automatic matching.

The currents of feminism

Feminism is not a single bloc: it brings together currents that agree neither on the causes of inequality nor on the ways to act. The same author can belong to several currents, and these definitions are simplifications. Click on a thinker from the list to read her profile.

Four “waves”, one way of marking the eras

Dividing history into waves is convenient but simplified: the currents coexist and respond to one another.

The debates

Questions on which feminists disagree. For each debate: first choose the position that sounds like you, then read each side's arguments in their strongest form, then see whether your position has changed. There is no expected right answer, and the positions are not ranked. Your answers stay in this browser.
